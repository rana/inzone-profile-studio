# Editing-signal studies

Six retained studies explore a distinction between Backspace and Delete while
keeping Esc signal red. Coral Backspace was physically tried and found too subtle
and too pale. It is archived as a result, with its exact colors and identity
preserved. That feedback does not establish a verdict for Coral Delete or either
ruby study.

**Rose Backspace**, using the exact Shift rose `#FF0080`, was applied for the
selected trial and its fresh export was verified. Physical feedback is pending;
that saved-data result does not establish preference or live device state.
**Orange Backspace** is a separate preview using the exact Insert orange
`#FF6A00`. Insert remains
unchanged: a hypothetical loss of emphasis is a question to observe, not a reason
to cascade changes into another accepted landmark.

## Exact options

Each study changes exactly one physical key from Amber Hinge + Rose. The inspected
US81 layout identifies Backspace as `BACKSPACE` (the wide 2-unit key) and Delete as
`DELETE` (the 1-unit key labeled Del). Both remove content in familiar editing
contexts; color cannot confirm an application's exact action or make either
operation universally safer. Esc stays `#FF0000`, as does the other editing key.
All other 80 keys retain the keeper colors, including all modifier pairs and
punctuation. These exact study artifacts are not combined with Operator Ice or
Three-Role Split.

## What the colors test

Rose and orange use familiar saturated colors instead of another small step
between pale-red shades. Rose Backspace intentionally shares the Shift hue;
Orange Backspace intentionally shares the Insert hue and sits close to the warm
F1–F4 bank. Whether those shared relationships are useful or confusing remains
unmeasured. Keep Insert, Delete and Esc unchanged while testing Backspace so the
result can answer that bounded question.

The earlier coral proposal widened a pale-red tint from `#FF4040` to `#FF6060`
after narrow amber differences had been difficult to see. The actual Coral
Backspace trial still found it too subtle and too pale. Do not repeat that
unresolved contrast prediction as evidence that another tint will work. The
original coral map remains recoverable under **Earlier versions** rather than
being silently recolored.

Ruby `#FF0060` is close to rose Shift `#FF0080`, so those roles may blend. It could
also remain too subtle against pure red. These are design risks, not findings
from a Ruby Backspace or Ruby Delete trial. The same caution applies to Coral
Delete; changing the key's position creates a different comparison.

## What would answer the question?

From typing distance, assess whether the two editing keys are distinguishable,
retain readable legends and fit the overall composition. Do not infer faster
editing, reduced errors, comfort or universal color psychology from RGB values
or a preference. Key size and room light can change apparent emphasis; 100%
requested brightness does not establish equal physical luminance.

All 81 keys stay illuminated. The public JSON files are palette designs, not
Sony native imports. Previewing, sharing and downloading do not write hardware.
A chosen trial needs the intended slot's fresh own export and backup, official
Sony import/Save, strict fresh-export comparison and separate physical feedback.
Keep the accepted keeper available for comparison.
